Personal Details

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam, commonly known as A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, was an Indian scientist, statesman, and the 11th President of India, serving from 2002 to 2007. He was renowned for his significant contributions to India's missile and nuclear weapons programs, earning him the title "Missile Man of India". He was also known for his work with the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), where he was instrumental in the development of India's satellite launch vehicle technology. Kalam's legacy extends beyond his scientific achievements, as he was widely admired for his inspirational speeches and dedication to education and youth development. Here's a more detailed look at his life and work:

Born on October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam earned a degree in aeronautical engineering from the Madras Institute of Technology. Scientific Career: He joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) in 1958 and later moved to ISRO, where he played a key role in developing India's missile and nuclear programs. Presidential Service: As the 11th President of India, Kalam served from 2002 to 2007 and was known for his simplicity and connection with the people, earning him the nickname "People's President". Inspiration and Legacy: Kalam's life and work continue to inspire generations, particularly among young people, with his emphasis on education, hard work, and perseverance. He was also a prolific writer, authoring several books on topics ranging from science to spirituality. Death: Kalam passed away on July 27, 2015, while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management in Shillong. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam |
